I've got a new lead

Account Executive

Leads are where everything starts. A lead is someone you think might be worth a conversation — it is not pipeline yet, and it should not be.

Click pathApp Launcher›Leads›Working Leads›open a lead
  1. Open Leads and pick the Working Leads list, or create a new one.
  2. Fill in How Did You Hear About Us. It is one click and it follows the deal all the way to close.
  3. Move the status to Warming when you start working it, then Connected once they reply.
  4. When a meeting goes in the diary, set the status to Meeting Set.

Salesforce does this for you

  • Stamps the date you set the meeting, and the date you held it — you never type either.
  • Logs your calls, emails and meetings against the lead automatically.
Try it Open Working Leads Meetings To Run

I've had the meeting

Account Executive

This is the decision point. Either it is worth pursuing, or it is not — and both answers are useful.

Click pathopen the lead›Convert — or set status to Disqualified
  1. Set the lead status to Meeting Held.
  2. If it is worth pursuing, click Convert. That creates the account, the contact and the opportunity in one go.
  3. If it is not, set the status to Disqualified and pick a Disqualification Reason.

Don't skip the reason

The lead is never deleted. Next time that company comes round — and they will — whoever picks it up can see you already spoke to them and why it didn't go anywhere. That is worth ten seconds now.

Example Arbor Fintech — ready to convert Pallas Media — disqualified

I'm moving a deal forward

Account Executive & Sales Engineer

The bar across the top of every opportunity is your map. Click a stage and it tells you what that stage needs.

Click pathOpportunities›open the deal›click a stage on the path›expand the chevron

Here is what each stage needs before you can leave it. Nothing else is enforced — fill in the rest as you learn it.

DiscoveryBusiness Qualification set to Qualified, and the Pain Point written down
Technical ValidationTechnical Qualification Qualified, BANT 75%+, plus Amount, Close Date and Champion
Proof of ConceptRecord the Tech Win result — yes or no, with a reason

Salesforce does this for you

  • Works out the BANT score — you just answer the four questions.
  • Records the date the stage changed, so days-in-stage is always right.
  • Fills in qualification answers from your Gong calls where it can. You can always overwrite them.

Salesforce won't let me move my deal

Everyone — read this one

This is deliberate, and the message always tells you exactly what is missing. Here is what it looks like:

Click pathchange the Stage›Save›read the message
If it saysDo this
Both qualification gates must be QualifiedSet Business Qualification yourself. Ask your SE to set Technical Qualification — it is their call, not yours.
BANT must be at least 75%You need three of the four marked Confirmed. Check the BANT section — Estimated doesn't count.
Amount must be populatedPut in your best number. It can change later.
Close Date must be populatedIt cannot be in the past.
Champion must be populatedPick the contact who is arguing for you internally.
Pain Point must be populatedWrite what is actually broken for them, in their words. No pain, no deal.
Pricing approval is requiredSubmit for approval and wait for the decision — see below.
Record a Win / Loss ReasonYou're closing it lost and haven't said why.

You can always close a deal lost

None of these gates stop you disqualifying or closing something out. They only apply to moving a deal forward. If a deal is dead, close it — that keeps the forecast honest.

I'm running a POC

Account Executive & Sales Engineer

Move the deal to Proof of Concept on the day the customer actually gets access — that starts the clock.

Click pathopen the deal›Details›Proof of Concept section
  1. Paste the link to your scoping doc. The full checklist stays in the doc, not in Salesforce.
  2. Write a short summary so nobody has to open the doc to understand the deal.
  3. Write the success criteria explicitly — what has to be true for this to count as a win.
  4. Set a target end date.
  5. When it finishes, set Tech Win to Yes or No, pick a reason, and add a note.

A technical loss doesn't kill the deal

Setting Tech Win to No does not close anything. Deals are still won on commercial grounds. It flags the opportunity so someone can decide what to do — that decision stays yours.

I need pricing approved

Account Executive

Once you've agreed a number, it goes to the CEO before the deal can move into Security Review.

Click pathopen the deal›Submit for Approval›wait for the decision›move the stage yourself
  1. Make sure the Amount on the opportunity is the number you actually agreed.
  2. Click Submit for Approval.
  3. You'll see the status change to Pending Approval, and the approver gets notified.
  4. Once approved, you move the deal to Security Review. Approval unlocks the move; it doesn't make it.

Salesforce does this for you

  • Records who approved it, when, and at what number.
  • Keeps the full approval history on the record, so nobody has to dig through Slack.
  • Lets you keep editing the deal while you wait — you are not locked out.

I've won the deal

Account Executive

Click pathopen the deal›Details›Contract Details›set Stage to Closed Won
  1. Fill in the Contract Details — start date, end date, term, payment terms, billing frequency.
  2. Set the stage to Closed Won.
  3. Add a Win Reason and a note on why you won, while it's fresh.

Then Salesforce does the rest

  • Creates next year's renewal opportunity, dated from the end of this contract.
  • Copies every product line onto it with the right dates. You don't rebuild the deal.
  • Posts the win to the team.
  • Tells the office manager to send the customer their swag, with the address.

Contract dates matter more than you'd think

The renewal is built from them. If the end date is wrong, next year's renewal lands on the wrong date and nobody notices until it's late.

I've lost the deal

Account Executive

  1. Set the stage to Closed Lost.
  2. Pick a Win / Loss Reason — this one is required.
  3. Add a note with anything worth knowing if we go back in.

Why the reason is compulsory

It is the only thing a win/loss review has to work with. "Competitor Won" with a note saying who and why is worth more than a tidy pipeline.

I want to sell more to a customer

Account Executive

Click paththe account›New Opportunity›record type Expansion›Co-Term with Renewal
  1. Create an opportunity on the account and set the record type to Expansion.
  2. Add the products they're buying.
  3. If they already have a renewal coming up, click Co-Term with Renewal.
  4. Pick the renewal. Done.

Do this rather than co-terming by hand

Lining up dates manually is where expansion revenue usually gets mis-dated — and it's the customer who notices, on their invoice.

What changed from Monica

Worth knowing

In MonicaNow
A booked meeting was an opportunityMeetings live on the lead. An opportunity is created once you've spoken to them and it's real — so pipeline actually means pipeline.
You could move a deal wheneverStages have requirements. The message always tells you what's missing.
